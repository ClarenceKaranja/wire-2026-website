# Content approval checklist

Use this before publishing anything. Everything published is public and may be copied, cached or indexed by search engines within minutes, even if it is removed later. An unlisted or "hidden" web address is **not** private.

## Never publish

- Restricted Access Meeting schedules, participant lists, room assignments or agendas
- Confidential operational or security information (movements, protection arrangements, transport plans for named people)
- Private participant contact details (personal emails, phone numbers, WhatsApp numbers)
- Passport details, dates of birth, visa or eTA documents, flight itineraries
- Participant lists or directories (the PAX list must never be uploaded to the Studio)
- Internal planning spreadsheets, budgets, procurement or contract records
- Internal or unapproved correspondence
- Drafts of documents that have not been cleared
- Logos or branding that are not the approved official files

## Before pressing Publish

- [ ] The information comes from an approved source document, not from memory or an email thread.
- [ ] Dates and times are correct and in Nairobi time.
- [ ] Names, titles and organizations are spelled as the person/organization wishes.
- [ ] Every person shown has agreed to appear (consent box ticked).
- [ ] Documents: the file is the cleared version, metadata (author, comments, tracked changes) removed, version label filled in.
- [ ] Photos: the communications focal point approved them; no badges, screens, documents or people who opted out are visible; alternative text written.
- [ ] Partner acknowledgements use the wording each partner approved.
- [ ] Links open the right page.
- [ ] Contacts are official or role-based, or the person agreed.

## If something was published by mistake

1. **Unpublish** it in the Studio straight away (menu next to Publish → Unpublish).
2. If the webhook is not set up, the page can stay visible for up to a minute.
3. If it was a **file** (PDF, photo), the file address still works for anyone who copied it. Ask the site administrator to delete the asset (see [maintenance guide](maintenance-and-handover.md#removing-a-file-completely)).
4. Tell the communications focal point and record what was exposed and for how long.
5. If personal data was exposed, follow UNODC's data protection incident procedure.
